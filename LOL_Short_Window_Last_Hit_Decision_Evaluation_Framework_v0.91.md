# LOL Short-Window Last-Hit Decision Evaluation Framework
## Analyst v0.911 · Standalone Pre-release
### A player-centered probabilistic event algorithm over a finite window

> **Scope:** A champion's minion targets, attack timing and action order within a wave or specified minion group.  
> **Window:** Starts at an explicit \(t_0\), usually covers 20–30 seconds, and ends at task settlement or the window boundary.  
> **Perspective:** Select actions for our champion while predicting the opponent's state, attacks on minions, and events generated by other units.  
> **Method:** Use state transitions, legal actions, probabilistic branches and event advancement to improve relative experience and gold acquisition efficiency while comparing both sides' short-term costs.  
> **Behavioral reference:** Professional matches can inform rules and validate targets, order and timing; the algorithm does not depend on a fixed collection of recordings.  
> **Version:** Generic data defaults to patch 26.20. Other patches require their corresponding data and mechanics.

---

# 0. Study Window and Task Boundaries

Analysis starts from a specified local state at \(t_0\). A typical task follows one wave from contact until its units die, approximately 20–30 seconds; a smaller group of critical low-health minions may also be selected.

At \(t_0\), specify the task-minion set \(\mathcal M^{task}\) and maximum planning horizon \(H\):

\[
T_{plan}=\min\{t_0+H,\ T_{task\ complete}\},\qquad T_{end}=t_0+H.
\]

Keep the task set explicit throughout an analysis. The core task may finish at \(T_{plan}\), but strategy scoring uses the shared \(T_{end}\), with identical rules for recovery and resource accounting over the remaining time. Minions entering later participate as environmental units in targeting and damage; a new task definition determines whether to include them in the next task.

The output is a conditional action chain and its event-trigger rules. Update predictions with observations; do not generalize one local chain into a policy for every moment.

# 1. Core Question

The model asks:

> Within the settlement window for the current minion group, which minion should we attack, when should we commit damage, when should we wait, and how should we preserve the next last hit?

Attacks include both killing blows and preparatory damage for later last hits. The objective is to improve achievable last-hit returns and execution stability while accounting for short-term costs.

Pure champion harassment belongs to free time and is not an independent core search action. Compare minion-action chains under the same conditional free-time policy, accounting for both champions' actual health, mana and ability occupation. An action that also changes minion health, aggro, position, or either champion's ability to attack minions is a coupled event.

The local objective for a candidate chain \(\pi\) is therefore

\[
J_{t_0:T}(\pi)=E\left[U_{t_0:T}(\pi)\mid\Omega_{t_0}\right]
-\lambda\operatorname{Risk}(L_{t_0:T}(\pi)),
\]

\(U\) aggregates gold, experience, unsettled resources and state costs. \(L\) represents losses such as missed last hits, disruption of the death order, or exceeding an exposure limit. The selected object is a finite-window action chain, not a fixed script for an entire match.

## 2.1 Recompute minion damage at every impact

Minion-versus-minion damage includes Minion Slayer's target-current-health component. Patch 26.9 sets the melee, caster and siege fractions to 2%, 3.5% and 5%, respectively, and changes melee base health to 430 with 35 added every 90 seconds. [Official 26.9 notes](https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/news/game-updates/league-of-legends-patch-26-9-notes/)

Damage for an individual impact is

\[
d_{j\to i}(u)=\operatorname{ResolveDamage}
\bigl(D_j^{AA}(u),\ \beta_{c_j}H_i(u^-),\ S(u^-),\ version\bigr).
\]

ResolveDamage applies patch-specific damage types, resistances and modifiers to each component. Base parameters may remain fixed between scaling boundaries; \(H_i(u^-)\) and its resulting damage must update with events rather than being frozen at 11, 19.5 or 36.

# 3. Player-centered Local State

Let our champion be A and the opposing champion B:

\[
S(t)=\{C_A,C_B,\mathcal M_A,\mathcal M_B,\mathcal U,G,\mathcal E,\Omega,W\}.
\]

\(\mathcal U\) contains relevant turrets, summons and other units that can affect task minions. \(G\) is the attack graph, \(\mathcal E\) the event queue, \(\Omega\) the uncertain-state and behavior scenarios, and \(W\) the task weights for this window.

The algorithm selects only A's actions. Model B with a state-dependent probabilistic policy; observed or committed actions are conditional inputs. Advance other units according to their attack, movement and targeting rules.

Maintain a state belief when observations are incomplete rather than forcing hidden fields to definite values:

\[
\Omega_{t+1}(s')\propto P(o_{t+1}\mid s')
\sum_s P(s'\mid s,a_t)\Omega_t(s).
\]

When health, identity, target or attack phase is known only as an interval or candidate set, advance each compatible branch separately. Remove incompatible branches as observations arrive.

# 4. Minion State

Record each minion as

\[
m_i=(id_i,c_i,s_i,H_i,H_i^{max},P_i,Z_i,\phi_i,K_i,B_i).
\]

\(id_i\) is its persistent identifier; \(c_i,s_i\) identify type and team; \(Z_i\) is its current target; \(\phi_i\) includes walking, windup, committed and recovery states; \(K_i\) contains patch and wave parameters; \(B_i\) contains buffs.

Retain intervals or candidate states when health, position, target or phase is unclear. Identity is also part of the belief state: do not renumber units by their left-to-right order after occlusion or crossing. Preserve wave, type and cross-frame identity candidates. Do not assign an uncertain death to a particular initial minion. Apply camera and scale handling before using screen coordinates in distance calculations; record health-bar fractions separately from absolute health.

# 5. Champion and Special-unit State

Champion state includes at least health and mana, level and XP, position and move speed, attack recovery, target-dependent damage, ability ranks and ready times, short-lived effects and special resources.

For champion \(h\), write

\[
C_h=(H_h,M_h,L_h,P_h,v_h,r_h,\mathcal R_h,\mathcal B_h),
\]

The fields represent health, resource, level/XP, position, move speed, attack recovery, available abilities, and buffs/debuffs. Record an inapplicable field as not applicable, not as zero.

| Mechanism | State relevant to last hitting |
|---|---|
| Ordinary basic attack | Windup, commitment time, period, range, projectile speed and target damage |
| Summon or special-unit attack | Existence, position, lifetime, creation/control resources, attack range and rhythm; remove from state and action sets when absent |
| Ranged or targeted ability | Range, cast time, damage, hit targets, additional effects and recovery/cooldown |
| Multi-stage ability | Each impact, return or second activation, and shared casting occupation |
| Non-champion or turret area ability | Position, ability events, turret target, next commitment and impact |

Describe other champions and units with the same fields and their actual event-generation rules. The algorithm does not depend on champion names, a particular wave or fixed attack counts.

Summons and special units are optional unit categories, not default components of every champion. If absent, related fields are not applicable and no summoning, attack or control event may be generated. Enable their rules only in the corresponding instantiated unit data.

# 6. Discrete Event Queue

\[
e_k=(id_k,t_k,source_k,target_k,type_k,status_k,parent_k).
\]

Events include arrival, attack initiation, damage commitment, impact, cancellation, death, retargeting, ability recovery, buff changes and level-ups. Calculate actual damage from state at impact.

Multiple targets or stages of one ability share \(parent_k\); charge mana and cooldown once. Retain feasible ordering branches when neighboring impact times cannot be distinguished.

# 7. Events Change Subsequent Events

\[
S(t^+)=F_{version}(S(t^-),e_t).
\]

When a minion dies, its uncommitted attacks become unavailable. Units attacking it select new targets, changing other minions' incoming damage and death times.

Preserve committed projectiles separately and check their actual rules. Retargeting, source death or target movement does not automatically cancel every related projectile.

# 8. Attack Graph

An edge \(j\to i\) means unit j attacks unit i. Record current relationships and the next transitions allowed by the rules.

Assign persistent identifiers by team, wave and type: for example, first-wave blue melee BM1–BM3 and casters BC1–BC3, with red RM1–RM3 and RC1–RC3. Establish identifiers from the first reliable reference frame. They do not denote current left/right order, front/back order or necessarily spawn order. Preserve them through crossing, overtaking and detours. Keep identity candidates during occlusion; proximity alone does not identify a previously tracked unit.

For B's attack on an allied minion, compute its health loss, B's attack or ability occupation, the firepower removed when it dies, and indirect effects on our last-hit targets.

Apply patch-specific targeting triggers, search ranges, pathfinding and movement time, retaining branches for multiple plausible targets. Selecting a target is not equivalent to attacking it. Distinguish search results, pursuit targets, actual windup targets and committed projectile targets. Aggro priority depends on valid actions and patch rules, not merely a nonempty target field.

For each minion, retain current target priority, validity, distance, reevaluation conditions, windup start and next ready time. Aggro signals generate candidates. At legal reevaluation times, compare candidates with the current target. Distance governs availability, selection among equal-priority candidates and attack arrival; closer does not necessarily mean retargeting. Parameterize initial acquisition, continued targeting and first-wave special behavior separately.

Advance through candidate target → selected target → pursuit or stop → windup → commitment → impact. Generate no windup outside actual attack range. Recheck state after loss of acquisition range, vision or windup cancellation. Target retention also requires validity. Handle committed projectiles independently of subsequent movement or retargeting.

# 9. Purpose of the Baseline

At the same \(t_0\), first calculate a baseline without additional preparatory damage from our champion, revealing natural focus fire and last-hit windows.

When evaluating setup, the baseline still permits normal later last hits. Both branches use the same last-hit rules and the same conditional opponent policy; different states may produce different responses.

The baseline explains what an action changes. Compare a few feasible routes relevant to the current last-hit problem rather than enumerating every possible input.

Let \(\pi_0\) omit our current additional coupled action while retaining normal last hitting. A candidate's local increment is

\[
\Delta J_t(a)=J(\pi_0\oplus a\mid\Omega_t)-J(\pi_0\mid\Omega_t).
\]

Prioritize it only when the increment is positive and risk, resource and next-last-hit constraints remain satisfied.

# 10. Death Sequence

\[
\mathcal T^\pi=\{T_i^{death,\pi}\}_{i\in\mathcal M^{task}}.
\]

Each policy \(\pi\) produces a probabilistic death sequence. This is an input to last-hit scheduling, not a requirement to attack at the final instant before death.

Treat death intervals as deadlines and attack recovery, cast locks, arrival and resource costs as shared constraints. A finite scheduling formulation is

\[
\max_\pi\sum_i V_i I_i(\pi)-\lambda C(\pi)-\mu R(\pi),
\]

\(I_i\) indicates whether minion \(i\) remains collectible, \(C\) is health, mana, cooldown and position cost, and \(R\) is last-hit or death-order risk. When successive health values or death times are unknown, report deadline intervals and conditional results only.

# 11. Last-hit Collisions

A small \(\Delta T_k=T_{(k+1)}^{death}-T_k^{death}\) can trigger a warning.

The final test uses both executable windows and shared action occupation. Near-simultaneous natural deaths may still be collectible by settling one earlier or combining an attack with an ability.

Movement, attack recovery and projectile flight may overlap on the actual timeline; do not mechanically add their durations.

# 12. Impact Time

An ordinary projectile basic attack can be approximated by

\[
T_{A,i}^{land}=s+\tau_A^{wind}+d(P_A,P_i)/v_A^{proj}.
\]

Here s is an executable initiation time. Melee attacks, summons or special units, line abilities and multi-stage abilities use their own impact functions.

A killing blow requires

\[
0<H_i(T_{A,i}^{land-})\le D_{A,i}(S(T_{A,i}^{land-})).
\]

Early commitment followed by later impact is a valid timing choice. A target above the lethal threshold at click time may still be collectible at impact.

# 13. Last-hit Windows

For a preceding action chain \(\pi\), minion i and last-hit method r:

\[
\mathcal L_{i,r}^{\pi}
=\{u:0<H_i^\pi(u^-)\le D_{r,i}(S^\pi(u^-))\}.
\]

\(S^\pi\) includes previous actions but not the candidate last-hit damage currently under evaluation.

The executable initiation set is

\[
\mathcal C_{i,r}^{\pi}
=\{s:Ready_r(s),Reachable_{i,r}(s),Executable(s,r,\pi),
T_{r,i}^{land}(s)\in\mathcal L_{i,r}^{\pi}\}.
\]

If nonempty, its right boundary gives that method's deadline. If empty, report infeasibility. Different methods may have different windows.

# 14. Uncertain Windows

For state and behavior scenario \(\omega\), compute

\[
p_{i,r}^{\pi}(s)=P_\omega(s\in\mathcal C_{i,r}^{\pi,\omega}).
\]

Jointly include current minion health, damage order, retargeting and opponent actions. Preserve correlations between events affected by one ability or the same retargeting event.

Report success probabilities and intervals only with a probabilistic basis. Without calibrated scenario weights, report conditional feasibility or input-range sensitivity. Uniform sample proportions are not professional behavior probabilities; equally weighted opponent branches are not an estimated opponent-policy distribution.

# 15. Joint Scheduling of Multiple Last Hits

Two minions being individually collectible does not imply both fit in one action chain. Jointly satisfy windup, attack period, cast occupation, movement and target-window constraints.

Start with the current target, next target and their key interacting units. After death, retargeting or opponent attacks, advance toward the task endpoint and update this related set.

If the earliest feasible initiation of submission k is \(s_k\) and its occupation lasts until \(f_k\), joint scheduling requires

\[
s_{k+1}\ge f_k,qquad
s_k\in\mathcal C_{i_k,r_k},qquad
f_k\le T_{i_k}^{death}
\]

as well as movement, casting and resource constraints. Failure makes the chain infeasible, not merely one isolated last hit.

# 16. Free Time

Free time consists of insertable actions outside the core minion plan that preserve its required last-hit feasibility:

\[
\mathcal F_A(\pi)=\{(s,a_{ext}):\pi\oplus(s,a_{ext})
\text{ still satisfies this window's last-hit and cost constraints}\}.
\]

Pure harassment is \(a_{ext}\). Account for its consequences under a shared conditional policy; the core algorithm selects the minion chain that creates this free time. If an inserted action changes minion state or either side's last-hit conditions, feed it back into the event queue and recompute. Free seconds are not already-realized damage or returns.

Free time may appear before the first last hit, between last hits or after group settlement; collecting several targets with one ability may enlarge it.

### Resource commitment and window conversion

A general laning principle is that committing an action at a predictable time to obtain a resource temporarily restricts the opponent's responses. Convert that commitment into harassment, displacement, earlier settlement or a wave-position change only while preserving our own critical last hits. This condition is independent of champion, ability or rune.

Define an indicator for opposing resource-commitment events:

\[
Claim_B(j,t)=\mathbf 1\{T^{commit}_{B,j}-t\le\tau_{claim}\land
\text{target remains collectible}\}.
\]

An attack or cast enters the core search only if it changes death time, the attack graph, opposing responses or our next-last-hit feasibility.

\[
Q_{claim}(a|S_t)=p_{exec}(a)\cdot\left[
\Delta R_A(a)+\mu_B\Delta C_B(a)+\rho\Delta T^{restricted}_B(a)
\right]-\lambda C_A(a)-\kappa R(a).
\]

\[
\Delta R_A=w_X\Delta X_A+w_G\Delta G_A,
\qquad
\Delta C_B=w_H\Delta H_B^{loss}+w_M\Delta M_B^{loss}
+w_{CD}\Delta CD_B+w_{pos}\Delta T_B^{forced}.
\]

\(C_A\) includes health, mana, cooldown, position and deadline costs. \(\Delta C_B\) contains observable or provable opposing state costs, not assumed damage treated as established returns. \(\Delta T_B^{restricted}\) is the feasible resource time lost because of health, position, cooldown or the wave.

The scoring principle is to exploit opposing resource commitments to improve the next collectible windows at a cost justified by the counterfactual benefit.

When the opponent has not committed, our critical deadline is narrow or our cost is high, prioritize waiting, moving or collecting resources. Retain probabilistic branches when alternatives are close.

This generalizes attacking during an opposing last hit into changing the next resource windows while the opponent is committed and while the benefit justifies our own cost. It covers last-hit pressure, harassment, displacement, trades and wave control.

# 17. Action Pressure

Action pressure combines target value, window width, distance and recovery. Narrower windows with fewer substitutes require more timely attention.

\[
\Phi_A(i,t)=\frac{V_i\,P(\text{miss a feasible window}\mid S(t))}
{\operatorname{width}(\mathcal C_{A,i})+\epsilon}.
\]

Define the opposing quantity analogously. Use it to schedule observation and search priorities, not to assume a pressured opponent must choose one action.

# 18. Last-hit Phase

Both sides' executable initiation sets determine their relative phase. A phase difference may let us collect first and then wait, or observe the opponent's commitment while our own window remains broad.

An opposing ability that kills an allied minion earlier changes this phase. Update it from event ordering; the first CS credit alone does not establish the better setup.

For champion \(h\) and target \(i\), define phase margin

\[
M_{h,i}(t)=T_{i}^{death}(t)-T_{h,i}^{ready}(t).
\]

\(M>0\) indicates theoretical arrival slack; \(M\le0\) requires a faster method, a target switch or abandoning that last hit. This is local feasibility, not the last-hit outcome itself.

# 19. Scope of Phase Adjustment

Attacking enemy minions changes our targets' health and may remove their firepower against allied minions earlier, changing the opponent's last-hit clock.

Movement and aggro manipulation change reachable targets and future incoming damage. Infer the direction from patch-specific events; do not assume an operation always advances or delays death.

Define the phase adjustment of action \(a\) by

\[
\Delta M_i(a)=M_i^{after}(a)-M_i^{before}(a),
\]

and evaluate it together with champion cost and next-target slack. Enlarging one target's margin alone does not imply a better overall result.

# 20. Effective Firepower

Represent short-term firepower by events that can actually reach the target:

\[
\mathcal E_i^{in}(t,T_{end})
=\{e:target(e)=i,\ e\text{ is executable in this branch}\}.
\]

Average DPS is a coarse filter. Final last-hit ordering uses windups, projectiles, target-current-health damage and retargeting events.

# 21. Early Removal of Damage Sources

Killing a damage-dealing minion early removes its uncommitted attacks and makes its attackers acquire new targets.

The benefit may be a wider last-hit window or a changed allied-minion lifetime. Evaluate only changes within the task window.

If action \(a\) removes damage source j early, the uncommitted incoming-event set changes by

\[
\Delta \mathcal E_i^{in}(a)=
\mathcal E_i^{in,after}(a)-\mathcal E_i^{in,before}(a),
\]

Use this together with target death times, our last-hit windows and action costs.

# 22. Distributing Preparatory Damage

Targets receiving distributed preparatory damage continue dealing damage in the short term. Its value depends on whether the resulting health layers are jointly schedulable.

Compare purposeful preparatory damage with preserving an attack; even health reduction is not an objective in itself.

For preparatory-damage vector \(q=(q_1,\ldots,q_n)\), retain only branches satisfying

\[
H_i-q_i\ge H_i^{safe},qquad
\mathcal C_{next}(q)\ne\varnothing
\]

where \(H_i^{safe}\) is the lower health boundary needed for later planned settlement.

# 23. Health Layering

A setup aims to settle related targets at different executable times while preserving slack for the next action.

Layering changes lethal-threshold entry, window width and recovery conflicts. Natural death spacing is only one indicator.

Represent the setup by a target-layer vector:

\[
L(t)=(H_1(t)-H_1^{kill},\ldots,H_n(t)-H_n^{kill}),
\]

\(H_i^{kill}\) is the lethal threshold for the current attack method. Prefer setups that enlarge the executable time separation between neighboring layers while preserving subsequent actions.

# 24. Preparatory Damage and Killing Blows

The same attack has two principal uses: change later windows if the target survives, or immediately settle gold and change the attack graph if it dies.

Determine the outcome in each impact branch and regenerate subsequent events.

Define an action-use indicator

\[
U_{use}(a)=
\begin{cases}
\Delta\operatorname{window}(a),&H_i^{after}>0,\\
V_i+\Delta\operatorname{graph}(a),&H_i^{after}\le0.
\end{cases}
\]

The first branch measures later-window improvement while the target survives; the second measures death-settlement value and graph changes. Do not count both for the same result.

# 25. Target Selection

Targets are legally reachable enemy minions in the current window, not entries in a fixed minion-type priority table.

First identify targets approaching the lethal threshold, under clear focus fire, involved in scheduling conflicts or carrying high value. Compare a few actions that can change settlement.

The legal target set is

\[
\mathcal I_t=\{i\in\mathcal M^{task}:Reachable_i(t)\land Visible_i(t)\land ValidTarget_i(t)\}.
\]

If a target is legal only in some belief branches, retain its branch probability rather than treating it as certainly attackable.

# 26. Target Screening Score

Use target scores to screen search candidates based on expected last-hit returns, task scheduling feasibility and attack cost.

Time differences, free time and phase explain how returns arise; do not reward them again when already represented by gold or fewer missed last hits.

A screening score for target \(i\) is

\[
Score_i=V_iP_i^{last-hit}
+\rho\,W_i^{next}
-\lambda C_i^{attack}
-\mu R_i^{deadline},
\]

\(P_i^{last-hit}\) is the probability of obtaining the killing blow in the current chain; \(W_i^{next}\) is the next-window contribution; \(C_i^{attack}\) is action cost; \(R_i^{deadline}\) is deadline risk. The screening score does not replace full chain value.

# 27. Relative Distance, Movement and Attack Reachability

Short-term prediction uses local relative positions without requiring absolute coordinates for the entire map. For each unit pair, record distance intervals, approach or retreat direction, current target, windup and recovery. A distance scalar alone cannot identify multi-target line hits; relative direction or feasible hit sets are also required.

Record distance, attack range, cast range, damage coverage and collision radius separately and align their measurement conventions for the action. Sprite edges, selection boxes, pathing radii and displayed ability indicators are not automatically impact boundaries. Champion attacks, summon creation/control/attacks and abilities have separate constraints. Also distinguish cast endpoints from damage paths.

Let \(d_{ij}(t)\) be distance under a consistent convention and \(R_{ij}^{(a)}\) the effective threshold for action \(a\). Arrival is

\[
t_{\rm reach}=\inf\{t\ge t_0:d_{ij}(t)\le R_{ij}^{(a)}\}.
\]

For a local straight-line interval with constant radial closing speed \(v_{\rm close}>0\), no attack stops and no turns, approximate it by

\[
\Delta t_{\rm reach}=\frac{\max(0,d_{ij}(t_0)-R_{ij}^{(a)})}{v_{\rm close}}.
\]

\(v_{\rm close}\) is the projection of both velocities onto the connecting direction, not always our move speed. Distinguish opposing movement, same-direction pursuit and a stationary attacking target. Recompute after turning, crowd control, attack initiation or target death. Use feasible path time when blocked.

If movement is permitted during attack recovery, initiation depends jointly on arrival, readiness and cast locks rather than their summed durations:

\[
t_{\rm start}\ge\max(t_{\rm reach},t_{\rm ready},t_{\rm unlocked}),\qquad
t_{\rm hit}=t_{\rm start}+t_{\rm windup}+t_{\rm flight}.
\]

During windup, check that the attack can still be committed. Advance committed projectiles under their tracking and invalidation rules. Minion pursuit, interrupted fire, retargeting and phase changes share the same event queue. Movement changes both our last-hit arrival and minion-versus-minion impact timing.

Between attack release and the next ready time, update relative position whenever movement is permitted; the whole attack cycle is not a stationary lock. Retreat changes enemy-minion pursuit distance, their ability to finish windups, and our next attack's arrival time. Evaluate all three together. Displacement, empowered forms, stacks and crowd control enter state transitions; waiting for stacks to expire is not a substitute for modeling the actual empowered action.

For recordings, calibrate scale using ground projection, camera changes and known-speed uninterrupted movement, then cross-check with confirmed range events. An attack need not occur at maximum range and provides only a range constraint. Never put uncalibrated pixels into game-unit formulas. Retain intervals for distance, direction and arrival when recovery is not unique.

## 27.1 Pixel observation coordinates

Record video measurements directly in pixels, using a continuously tracked map-texture landmark as the origin and each unit's health-bar center as its observation marker. Let \(b_i(t)\) be the bar center and \(o(t)\) the landmark's coordinates in the current frame:

\[
p_i^{px}(t)=b_i(t)-o(t),\qquad d_{ij}^{px}(t)=\|b_i(t)-b_j(t)\|_2.
\]

Between actions, record \(p_i^{px}(t_2)-p_i^{px}(t_1)\), \(d_{ij}^{px}(t_2)-d_{ij}^{px}(t_1)\) and \(t_2-t_1\). Same-frame pair distance does not require the origin; cross-frame displacement requires landmark tracking and a consistent camera scale. Without scale and bar-offset calibration, pixels describe relative image changes and must not be substituted into game-unit attack-range, cast-range or movement-speed formulas. Preserve unknown centers and origins, and label interpolation or estimates separately.

# 28. Role of Minion Types

Melee, caster and siege minions differ in health, range, windup and minion-damage rules. Their current positions and targets determine their actual role in the local event chain.

A siege minion's larger settlement value increases the weight on avoiding its loss; compute incoming damage and last-hit windows event by event.

# 29. Waiting Is a Decision

Output Wait or Move when attacking now would occupy the cycle needed for the next target, or damage would arrive before its lethal window opens.

Specify a trigger, such as recomputing after a particular projectile lands or committing when the target enters this attack's feasible impact set, rather than a fixed one-second pause.

Waiting can also acquire information:

\[
\operatorname{VOI}(o)=E[\max_aJ(a\mid o)]-\max_aE[J(a)].
\]

Retain waiting only when expected information value exceeds shrinking-deadline and missed-last-hit costs.

# 30. Commitment and Cancellation

Cancellation before commitment may preserve later choices. After commitment, advance damage and recovery under the actual rules.

Track initiation, commitment, movable time, readiness and impact separately. Impact is not the restart point for every resource timer.

Put committed attacks and abilities into an action-reservation schedule. New actions must check conflicts with existing windups, projectiles, cast locks and recovery. Retain hit-set branches when multi-target impact is uncertain; do not turn one ability into multiple confirmed hits.

# 31. Boundary of Behavioral Responses

The opponent may respond to our preparatory damage, position and abilities through \(P(b\mid\Omega,a)\).

Feints or induced responses enter core comparison only when they demonstrably change the minion-attack event chain. Other activity belongs to free time.

# 32. Core Action Set

\[
\mathcal A_M=\{Wait,Move,AA(i),Spell(i),AreaSpell(\mathcal I),
Setup(\mathcal I),Coupled(a)\}.
\]

i is an enemy minion and \(\mathcal I\) a related target set. Coupled denotes an action that changes both champion and minion states, or changes a champion's ability to attack minions.

Filter candidates to the current window. General action and event rules generate them; professional recordings provide optional search priors and timing references, not a fixed script as the sole algorithm.

# 33. Basic-attack Route

For each urgent target, first check whether one basic attack can reliably last-hit and whether its cycle is compatible with the next target.

Retain this route when it meets the task at lower cost. Each champion's actual attack mechanism uses its own event rules.

The feasible basic-attack set is

\[
\mathcal C_{AA}(i)=\{s:T_{s}^{hit}\le T_i^{death},\ d_{A i}(s)\le R_{AA},\ Ready_{AA}(s)=1\}.
\]

If empty, switch to an ability, movement or abandoning that last hit; do not keep an infeasible attack as a candidate.

# 34. Ability Route

When attack distance, settlement time or cycle is unsuitable, inspect currently ready or soon-ready abilities.

An ability may hit earlier, farther away or with different geometry, but incurs mana, cast occupation and next-target consequences.

Its route satisfies

\[
\mathcal C_{spell}(i)=\{(s,r):Ready_r(s)=1,\ d_{Ai}(s)\le R_r,
T_{r,i}^{hit}\le T_i^{death},\ M_r(s)\ge cost_r\}.
\]

\(M_r\) is the required resource. Carry cast occupation and cooldown into subsequent state.

# 35. Area Effects and Joint Settlement

Area abilities may last-hit multiple minions or collect one while affecting a champion. Generate events from the actual cast position, direction and target set.

Share the cost of one cast while settling each target. Evaluate saved attacks, additional free time and avoided cycle conflicts.

For target set \(\mathcal I\), area-ability value is

\[
J_{area}(r,\mathcal I)=\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}V_iP_i^{hit}
-Cost_r-\lambda\,Conflict_r(\mathcal I),
\]

\(Conflict_r\) captures conflicts imposed on the next last hit and movement route by cast occupation.

# 36. Selecting Initiation Time

Within executable sets, prefer timing with greater tolerance, next-target compatibility and lower cost:

\[
(i^*,r^*,s^*)\in\arg\max_{(i,r,s)\in\mathcal C}
J(i,r,s\mid\Omega_t,T_{end}).
\]

Immediate collection, early commitment with later impact, delayed collection and waiting for an ability are choices produced by windows. Current health percentage alone does not select initiation.

# 37. Delaying Death through Movement

When a patch-legal coupled action turns a low-health target away from some incoming attacks, regenerate its incoming event set.

If this preserves our executable last hit at acceptable movement cost, it has last-hit value. Determine the effect branch by branch and handle already-committed damage separately.

The local movement transition is

\[
S_{t+\Delta t}=F_{version}(S_t,Move(v,\Delta t)),
\]

Recompute minion targets, arrival times, attack phases and death sequences.

# 38. Opponent Actions Change Our Last-hit Conditions

Attacks on our champion or minions may change allied-minion targets, recovery and group position.

Enter these observations as environmental events. When trying to induce a response, retain noncooperative branches; value comes from the consequences for our last-hit feasibility.

# 39. Admission of Coupled Actions

An action enters core search if it changes task-minion health, the attack graph, minion position or incoming events, or either side's timing, range or executable method for attacking task minions.

Otherwise, output only its free-time and resource constraints. Do not sacrifice the window's principal collection task merely to increase pure harassment damage.

Define the admission indicator

\[
I_{coupled}(a)=\mathbf1\{\Delta H_i\ne0\ \lor\ \Delta G\ne0\ \lor\ \Delta Z\ne0\ \lor\ \Delta\mathcal C_A\ne\varnothing\}.
\]

Only \(I_{coupled}(a)=1\) admits an action to core last-hit search; otherwise treat it as a free-time action.

# 40. Task Weights for the Window

\(W\) specifies weights for XP and gold acquisition, both sides' resource costs and execution risk. Record gold and XP separately. Check clear dominance before applying published weights to routes with trade-offs.

Fix a shared endpoint \(T=t_0+H\), typically H = 20–30 seconds. Record early completion time but settle endpoint accounts under the same rules. Strategies cannot choose favorable denominators.

\[
\eta_X^A=\frac{X_A(T)-X_A(t_0)}{H},\qquad
\eta_G^A=\frac{G_A(T)-G_A(t_0)}{H}.
\]

Focus on \(\eta_X^A-\eta_X^B\), \(\eta_G^A-\eta_G^B\), and the costs both sides pay. This model does not plan long-term recalls, rotations or sustained freezes.

# 41. How Weights Affect Behavior

A higher penalty for missing valuable minions preserves resources earlier. Higher mana cost favors reliable attack routes; higher exposure cost favors ranged abilities or safer positions.

Use the same weights for the corresponding class of local states. Do not adjust each recording's weights to force a desired action from its outcome.

For resource/cost vector \(R(\pi)\) and weights \(w\),

\[
J_w(\pi)=w^{\mathsf T}R(\pi),
\qquad
\frac{\partial J_w}{\partial w_k}=R_k(\pi).
\]

Recheck ordering after changing weights. A route is robust to a weight when it remains optimal over an interval of that weight.

# 42. Probabilistic Opponent Policy

Principal branches include attacking a last-hit target, preparatory damage, ability settlement, movement/waiting and conceding the current last hit. Include other behavior coupled to those events as environmental branches.

\[
P(b\mid\Omega_t,a,version)
\]

depends on opposing recovery, abilities, position, target windows and observed behavior. Advance committed actions first; branch only on choices not yet determined.

A noisy inverse-planning form is

\[
P(b_t\mid\Omega_t,a_t)\propto
\exp\bigl(\theta_B^{\mathsf T}f(\Omega_t,b_t)\bigr).
\]

Features include target windows, attack intervals, movement distance, resource cost and next-event risk. Parameters express behavioral compatibility, not automatically player skill or optimality.

# 43. State Belief

\(\Omega_t\) is our observation-based belief about the true local state.

\[
\Omega_{t^+}\propto P(o_{t^+}\mid S_{t^+})
\,P(S_{t^+}\mid\Omega_t,a_t).
\]

Retain uncertainty in health reading, occlusion, unknown abilities and attack phase. On observing an event, discard incompatible branches and recompute.

# 44. Relative Resources and State Expenditure

## 44.1 Separate returns and costs

\[
U_A=w_G\frac{\Delta G_A-\mu_G\Delta G_B}{G_{ref}}
+w_X\frac{\Delta X_A-\mu_X\Delta X_B}{X_{ref}}
-C_A+\mu_C C_B+V_{boundary}.
\]

\(\Delta G\) is lane gold received in the window and \(\Delta X\) actual XP received. Fix reference scales and weights before comparing strategies. Preserve raw gold, XP, health and seconds before converting them into a score. This evaluates local resource advantage, not whole-match win probability.

\[
C_p=w_H\frac{H_p^{spent}}{H_{ref,p}}
+w_P\frac{P_p^{spent}}{P_{ref,p}}
+\sum_k w_{K,k}\frac{B_{p,k}}{H}.
\]

Report gross damage, healing and net health expenditure, but choose one for the main cost score to avoid double penalties. Track gross mana spending, restoration and endpoint balance separately. Mana-less champions have no mana-cost component. \(B_{p,k}\) is the time an ability or charge is unavailable in the window; recovery, stored charges and remaining availability still constrain legal actions. Do not penalize a cooldown opportunity cost again if the same loss is already represented by later missed last hits.

A chain may create advantage by saving our attacks/abilities, reducing cheap opposing collection routes or forcing a costlier collection method. Branch on concession and expenditure; do not assume the opponent must pay.

## 44.2 Received XP and unsettled resources

At minion death, settle XP using patch values, range, survival and sharing rules. A killing blow is not required for XP. Settle gold under the actual last-hit and gold rules.

Level-ups require accumulated received XP to reach the patch threshold; they are not fixed to a CS count or a particular death in a wave. Ordinary Summoner's Rift data for 25.6, 26.17 and 26.18 requires 280 XP for level two. Melee/caster solo XP is 61.75/30.4 in 25.6 and 62/31 in 26.17 and 26.18. A full first wave of three melee and three casters provides 276.45 or 279, insufficient for level two. If a second-wave melee dies before the last first-wave caster, four melee plus two casters provide 307.8 or 310 from only six deaths, enough to level. The surviving caster remains unsettled; six CS does not establish a missed last hit. Sum death XP and killing-blow gold separately by unit identity. A missed last hit may still give XP, and equal six-CS totals may yield different gold by composition. [Official 26.1 notes](https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/news/game-updates/patch-26-1-notes/), [25.6 client XP curve](https://raw.communitydragon.org/15.6/game/data/maps/shipping/map11/map11.bin.json), [26.18 client XP curve](https://raw.communitydragon.org/16.18/game/data/maps/shipping/map11/map11.bin.json).

Each task minion is alive/unsettled, dead with a resource credited to a side, or dead without the relevant champion receiving that resource. Surviving endpoint minions do not establish permanent opposing gold or XP loss.

Besides endpoint XP, report receipt curves and timing advantage:

\[
A_X=\frac{1}{H}\int_{t_0}^{T}
\left[(X_A(t)-X_A(t_0))-(X_B(t)-X_B(t_0))\right]dt.
\]

This measures earlier possession of XP. If scored, assign an independent weight and distinguish it from endpoint XP. Level-up transitions change abilities and attributes; do not add an unsupported bonus for the same XP again.

## 44.3 Window boundary and position evaluation

\(V_{boundary}\) records or estimates collectible resources of living minions, remaining champion state and committed events. Survival past the endpoint is not a fixed reward, and unsettled resources are not already received. Without a credible terminal valuation, report an unsettled-resource list and sensitivity rather than claiming permanent denial.

Chess offers the structure of legal moves, transitions, opposing responses and finite search. Allied minions are current firepower and resources for the opponent. Their survival value depends on subsequent events; preserving them is not automatically valuable as preserving a chess piece might be.

## 44.4 Settlement and cost conventions across similar windows

Record both champions' level-up or task-completion times and specify a fixed observation endpoint. Keep resource differences, timing differences and state costs separate. Counterfactual branches from one initial state must share a predetermined endpoint. Actual matches settled around corresponding events provide event-aligned descriptive comparisons only.

If only remaining health is visible, report deficit \(D_p(t)=H_{\max,p}(t)-H_p(t)\) and its net increase \(D_p(T)-D_p(t_0)\), not cumulative damage. Separately record initial deficits, maximum-health changes from levels or runes, recovery and potion use. Mana balances likewise are not total casting costs. Preserve unknown fields; do not infer them from CS, level or assumed player skill.

Across windows, prioritize return–cost vectors and visible dominance. More gold may accompany greater health or mana deficits; earlier completion may accompany fewer killing blows. Different patches, runes, items or initial waves do not directly establish causal action returns. Compare legal actions and responses within one state, then test whether the same principle distinguishes receipts and costs in similar windows.

## 44.5 Runes, items and post-hit state evolution

Both rune/item configurations and their patches belong to the initial state. Track trigger conditions, current stacks, cooldowns, durations, applicable damage/healing types and health thresholds, updating after each event. Check attacks on minions, attacks on champions and abilities hitting both separately. Effects first alter transitions and legal actions, then return–cost accounts.

Evaluate approach, initiation and withdrawal as one process, including damage taken, shielding, effective healing and ability occupation. Apply actual refresh and stacking rules; exclude overhealing. Record recovery completing outside the window as pending. Immediate loss affects current survival and feasibility; delayed recovery affects later state. Neither substitutes for the other, and healing already included in net health cost earns no duplicate reward.

For example, model Second Wind's damage trigger and continuing recovery, Doran's Shield's conditional recovery, and Revitalize's applicability to individual healing/shielding effects separately. Do not apply a universal healing multiplier. Check Absolute Focus against health before each damage event; advance Scorch through eligibility, cooldown and delayed damage; advance Press the Attack through valid champion hits and patch interactions. Preserve hidden-trigger branches rather than inferring contribution from rune selection or endpoint health.

At a last-hit node, compare immediate approach, delayed approach, an ability and concession, then advance through the related target and next-target settlements. Waiting depends on whether collection remains open, opposing responses become more expensive and the next target conflicts. Early attacks depend on death order, execution slack and exposure. Professional matches provide inspectable candidate actions and event order, not a skill-based return bonus or proof of optimality.

Update damage, recovery, stacks and cooldowns by events:

\[
x(t^+)=F_{version}\bigl(x(t^-),e_t\bigr),
\qquad
x=(H,M,CD,Stack,Buff).
\]

Preserve multiple \(F_{version}\) branches for invisible triggers until observations or rules exclude them.

### Rune eligibility, frequency and trigger success

Equipping a rune is not sufficient for a reward. For each champion \(h\) and rune \(r\) in \(W=[t_0,t_1]\), record eligible opportunities, actual triggers, cooldown occupation and marginal contribution:

\[
N^{eligible}_{r,h}=\#\{e\in W:\; Ready_r(e)=1\land Condition_r(e)=1\},
\]

\[
N^{trigger}_{r,h}=\#\{e\in W:\; Trigger_r(e)=1\},\qquad
p^{trigger}_{r,h}=\frac{N^{trigger}_{r,h}}{N^{eligible}_{r,h}+\epsilon},
\]

\[
f_{r,h}=\frac{N^{trigger}_{r,h}}{t_1-t_0},\qquad
A_{r,h}=1-\frac{T^{cooldown}_{r,h}}{t_1-t_0}.
\]

\(N^{eligible}\) counts events with conditions satisfied and the rune ready. \(N^{trigger}\) counts triggers confirmed by images, combat text, state changes or patch rules. \(p^{trigger}\) is conditional success, \(f\) triggers per unit time, and \(A\) availability. Update cooldowns, stacks, duration and multi-target events under the rune's patch rules; never count one trigger twice.

For the same state, action and opposing response, write its counterfactual marginal value as

\[
\Delta U_{r,h}=E[U\mid r\ active,\;a_h,\;s_t]
             -E[U\mid r\ disabled,\;a_h,\;s_t].
\]

Use the combination of \(p^{trigger}\), \(f\), \(A\) and \(\Delta U\). More triggers are not automatically better without improved windows, resources or costs. Retain bounds or candidate branches when uncertain; do not infer triggers from configuration, one damage number or endpoint health. With \(N^{eligible}=0\), success is undefined, not zero.

Each action-record row retains at least rune, event time, eligibility, readiness, confirmation, evidence, post-trigger cooldown, \(N^{eligible}\), \(N^{trigger}\), \(p^{trigger}\), \(f\), \(A\), \(\Delta U\) and evidence confidence. Rune metrics first enter transitions and then resource/risk evaluation. They do not replace position, attack intervals or death order.

### Passive resources and conversion

Treat each passive as a patch-dependent state mechanism, not a fixed bonus. State includes current stacks, cap, gain and consumption events, trigger conditions, duration and remaining returns.

Distinguish accumulation through movement/hits/kills, threshold conversion into shielding/AP/attack speed or another effect, and consumption through damage/casting/attacking/expiry. Check walking, dashes, knockbacks, Flash and Teleport separately under the applicable rules; movement alone does not imply accumulation.

\[
P_h(t^+)=F_{P,h,\mathrm{version}}\bigl(P_h(t^-),e_t\bigr),
\qquad
P_h=(q_h,q_{max,h},G_h,T^{ready}_h,T^{expire}_h,Trigger_h).
\]

\[
\Delta U_{passive}(a)=\Delta R_A^{passive}(a)+\mu\Delta C_B^{passive}(a)-C_A^{passive}(a).
\]

#### Event-by-event passive updates

Separate accumulation, threshold triggering and consumption:

\[
q_h(t^+)=\min\left(q_{max,h},q_h(t^-)+g_h(e_t)\right),
\qquad e_t\in\mathcal E^{gain}_h.
\]

\[
I^{trigger}_h(e_t)=\mathbf 1\left\{q_h(t^-)=q_{max,h}\land Condition_h(e_t)=1\right\},
\]

\[
q_h(t^+)=q_h(t^-)-c_h(e_t)I^{trigger}_h(e_t),
\qquad
B_h(t^+)=B_h(t^-)+b_h(e_t)I^{trigger}_h(e_t).
\]

If triggering creates a shield, buff or other temporary effect, update its expiry in the same event:

\[
T^{expire}_h(t^+)=t+\tau_h(e_t)\quad\text{if }I^{trigger}_h(e_t)=1.
\]

\[
p^{passive}_h=\frac{N^{trigger}_h}{N^{eligible}_h+\epsilon},
\qquad
f^{passive}_h=\frac{N^{trigger}_h}{T_{window}},
\]

\[
\Delta U^{passive}_{h,t}=p^{passive}_h\left(
 w_X\Delta X_h+w_G\Delta G_h+\mu\Delta C_{opponent,h}
\right)-C_{self,h}.
\]

\(g_h\) is gain, \(c_h\) consumption, \(b_h\) the resulting buff/shield, and \(N^{eligible}\) the events satisfying trigger conditions with the resource available. Selecting a champion does not itself earn passive credit; credit actual marginal effects on last hits, XP, gold and both sides' costs.

Write passive effects on timing back into the event chain: killing a minion with an ability for accumulation, preserving movement to prepare a shield, or accepting low-value damage near a shield refresh. Record accumulation, trigger success, duration and window effects, not merely ability use.

# 45. Risk Adjustment

\[
J(\pi)=E_{\Omega,b}[U(\pi)]
-\lambda\operatorname{CVaR}_{\alpha}(L(\pi)).
\]

Tail-loss constraints limit rare severe failures for valuable siege minions, consecutive last hits and high-exposure routes.

# 46. Attack or Preserve the Opportunity

Commit an attack when its risk-adjusted route value exceeds preservation by the decision margin \(\delta\).

If the advantage is insufficient or the window has not formed, preserve attack availability and specify the next recomputation event. Routes with small probability differences may coexist in the acceptable policy set.

# 47. Executability Filter

Exclude routes violating patch rules, reachability, resources, action occupation or an explicit exposure limit.

Check constraints protecting the next valuable last hit over the complete branch, including ability substitutes and coupled effects. An insufficient baseline attack must not exclude an effective retreat-and-ability route prematurely.

The legality indicator for route \(\pi\) is

\[
\operatorname{Legal}(\pi\mid\Omega_t)=
\mathbf1\{\text{version, range, resource, occupation and deadline constraints all hold}\}.
\]

Rank only routes with \(\operatorname{Legal}=1\).

# 48. Finite-policy and Game-theoretic Search

Identify the relevant last hits and generate a small set of actions or conditional chains changing their settlement. Merge outcome-equivalent branches. Prioritize effective focus fire, window conflicts and timing patterns common in professional play. Policies must adapt after observing a new state rather than replay only fixed timestamps.

An event kernel such as [SimPy](https://simpy.readthedocs.io/en/stable/) executes supplied mechanics; it does not supply missing game rules. Use common-initial-state rollout or expectation search for finite candidates. Multi-step partial-observation branches can use the belief-search structure of [pomdp_py POUCT/POMCP](https://h2r.github.io/pomdp-py/html/api/pomdp_py.algorithms.html). Committed opposing actions cannot be selected again.

For small candidate sets, form an own-policy × opposing-conditional-policy payoff matrix. [Nashpy](https://nashpy.readthedocs.io/en/stable/) or [PyGambit](https://gambitproject.readthedocs.io/en/stable/pygambit.html) can inspect best responses and mixtures. Under an explicit zero-sum relative-resource proxy, maximin gives a guarantee against the worst included response. General-sum objectives retain both payoffs separately.

Matrix optimality or equilibrium applies only to included policies. [OpenSpiel](https://openspiel.readthedocs.io/en/latest/algorithms.html) algorithms such as MCTS, information-set search and CFR also require complete legality and observation rules. A library is not evidence of correct mechanics or professional-behavior agreement.

Finite search can be written as

\[
V_t(\Omega_t)=\max_{a\in\mathcal A_M(\Omega_t)}
\left[r(\Omega_t,a)+E\bigl[V_{t+1}(\Omega_{t+1})\mid\Omega_t,a\bigr]\right],
\]

Stop at the shared endpoint or task completion. In practice, expand only finite branches relevant to the current target, next target and key interacting units.

# 49. Player-centered Probabilistic Event Algorithm

## Step 1: Establish the window

Select A, task minions, \(t_0\), horizon H and termination conditions. Typical H is 20–30 seconds.

## Step 2: Read pre-action state

Construct \(\Omega_{t_0}\) only from currently observed information, including opposing state, relevant units and committed events.

## Step 3: Generate baseline windows

Advance current focus fire and opposing minion-attack policies to obtain collectible windows without additional own preparatory damage.

## Step 4: Identify current and next targets

Use value, urgency and joint conflicts to select a few targets whose settlement needs to change.

## Step 5: Generate own core candidates

Generate legal attacks, abilities, area settlement, preparatory damage, waiting and movement routes. Retain coupled actions admitted by Section 39.

## Step 6: Advance other units

Branch on undecided opposing minion attacks and environmental events. Put committed attacks into the queue first and compute actual damage at impact.

## Step 7: Advance to the next decision event

Update both sides' windows at death, retargeting, recovery and arrival nodes. Retain state/response uncertainty and continue toward the endpoint.

## Step 8: Compute short-term value

Settle both sides' gold, XP receipt, living unsettled resources and health/mana/cooldown costs separately. Compare relative efficiency over a fixed window and reject occupation conflicts and limit violations.

## Online mode: Choose before the action

Repeat these steps in first-person or ongoing play. Future deaths, gold and level-ups are unavailable; use only the present screen, committed events and patch rules. After observation \(o_t\):

1. Update \(\Omega_t\), retaining uncertain targets, health and attack-phase branches.
2. Identify protected task minions, the nearest deadline and next recomputation trigger.
3. Generate a few legal \(a\in\mathcal A_M(\Omega_t)\): immediate attack, preparatory damage, arrival movement, waiting, withdrawal or concession.
4. Advance each candidate to the next key event over opponent-policy and environmental branches.
5. Choose a risk-adjusted action, not an action already observed:

\[
Q_t(a)=E[U_{t:T}(a)\mid\Omega_t]
-\lambda\operatorname{CVaR}_{\alpha}(L_{t:T}(a))
+\nu\operatorname{VOI}_t(a)
-\kappa C_{\rm execute}(a).
\]

\(C_{\rm execute}\) includes input delay, distance-estimation error, action locks and difficulty. \(\operatorname{VOI}\) is information from waiting or probing. Average over identity candidates when unknown. An optimistic high-value branch alone cannot justify an action whose safety is unproved.

If the two highest scores differ by less than \(\delta\), output waiting or information gathering with a trigger. Otherwise output the current target, method, commitment interval and cancellation conditions. Return immediately to Step 2 after execution; do not lock a 20–30 second script. \(Q_t\) is a pre-action prescription and cannot be inferred from future results. Section 51's behavioral losses are post-action validation only.

## Step 9: Select target, method and timing

Output higher-value, sufficiently tolerant action distributions or acceptable sets, with commitment and cancellation conditions.

## Step 10: Output free time

Report insertable time outside the core chain. Use a shared conditional policy for pure harassment and account for its resources; recompute if it changes last-hit conditions.

## Step 11: Execute the current action and update

Commit only one step. Update beliefs from new observations, retain the endpoint and advance through remaining task events.

## Step 12: Compare with professional behavior

Save algorithm output from the pre-action state, then compare actual targets, order and commitment timing. Visible settlement validates event advancement. Later actions are not mandatory earlier inputs.

# 50. Why Advance by Events

Half-second sampling can cover overall change; near-ordering of windup, commitment, projectile and death requires finer event time.

Update windows and beliefs after triggers and recompute within the same finite task. A horizon covering the local wave chain does not imply equal certainty at every moment.

The next event is

\[
e_{next}=\arg\min_{e\in\mathcal E_{pending}}t(e)
\]

Order simultaneous events by patch priorities and commitment relationships, update state and regenerate candidates.

# 51. Professional Behavioral References and Credibility

Validate both champions' minion actions: targets or target sets, attacks versus abilities, preparatory-hit counts, attack/wait order, commitment times, and entry into or exit from effective range. Identical killing-blow outcomes or cumulative CS are not mandatory for behavioral agreement.

\[
\ell_{behavior}=w_{target}\ell_{target}+w_{type}\ell_{type}
+w_{order}\ell_{order}+w_{time}\ell_{time}+w_{wait}\ell_{wait}.
\]

Compare timing within the same phase: windup start, projectile release and impact separately. A prediction inside an observed interval is compatible. Do not equate impact and commitment. Label ordinary attacks, optional special-unit attacks and abilities separately. When identities cannot be tracked, compare confirmed types or candidate sets without forcing a unique identifier.

Waiting deliberately preserves an attack opportunity. Label it only with sufficient continuous footage establishing readiness without commitment. Missing effects, no gold text or no confirmed event between two screenshots does not prove waiting. Infer approach/retreat from relative-distance changes without requiring absolute-coordinate replication.

Gold or CS changes alone are settlement observations, not action labels. Validate visible actions regardless of last-hit success. Pure champion harassment is external free-time behavior; attacks or abilities also changing minions remain in the main chain.

Given true state and committed actions, damage and killing blows follow mechanics. Incomplete observation, input delay and execution error produce conditional predictions:

\[
P(Y\mid o_{0:t},a)=\int P(Y\mid s,a,\epsilon)\,p(s,\epsilon\mid o_{0:t})\,ds\,d\epsilon.
\]

\(Y\) includes damage, death, gold and XP; \(\epsilon\) is execution/timing error. Last-hit success is not an independent random draw unrelated to health, damage and arrival order. Conditional return–cost distributions guide choices, but one differing outcome does not directly invalidate a strategy. Repeated impossible ranges, cooldowns, damage or death orders indicate incompatible transitions.

Full-observation replay checks accounting; continuous actions can calibrate hidden state; later actions or independent windows test prediction. Do not fit CS difference or killing-blow outcomes. Assigning the actual action high pre-action probability consistently across independent observations supports credibility. Distance losses and equal-weight scenario scans establish compatibility, not calibrated probability.

Sparse labels test whether confirmed actions are explained; they cannot establish absence of extra attacks. Score missed and falsely predicted actions separately within continuously labeled intervals. Report target, order, timing, waiting and relative distance separately. Half-second images cannot prove hundredth-second window differences.

Joint validation reports champion-action agreement, relative-movement direction, simultaneous minion-target/phase agreement and coverage for each. Under a 70% acceptance threshold, all three must meet it with sufficient continuous coverage. Static frames or a single high component cannot inflate the total. Freeze timing tolerance before tuning and specify windup, release or impact. A 0.3-second sample grid defines observation intervals, not the internal event timestep.

Paths or actions supplied from images are conditions, not successful predictions. Mark occlusion, identity crossing and unclear animation as unknown and include them in coverage denominators; simulation output cannot replace observed labels. Text, names, gold and CS do not enter action fitting. Unit bodies, bar shapes, relative movement and projectiles are visual observations; estimate camera motion separately.

## 51.1 Blind evaluation and the boundary of skill scoring

Before calculation, the scorer must not read player names, ranks, commentary conclusions, recording identifiers or supplied skill labels. Inputs are patch, champions/initial state, runes/items, window observations, actions and visible resource results. Otherwise labels can leak through \(P(b\mid\Omega,a)\), candidate weights or explanations.

Score champions separately. For \(h\), distinguish three quantities:

\[
S_h^{decision}=E[Q_t(a_h^*)]-E[Q_t(a_h^{alt})],
\]

the action's decision advantage relative to a feasible alternative;

\[
S_h^{behavior}=1-\ell_h^{behavior},
\]

compatibility with a fixed behavioral reference; and

\[
C_h=\operatorname{coverage}_h\times\operatorname{identifiability}_h
\]

evidence confidence. Do not merge them into an unexplained player-skill score. Without independent professional-reference samples, \(S_h^{decision}\) can describe short-window decision quality but not overall player ability.

To map windows to relative skill, freeze rules before leave-one-clip or leave-one-match-out validation. Reference clips must not tune a target clip's weights, thresholds or parameters. Report both sides' scores, confidence, coverage and unknowns. Low coverage weakens conclusions; it is not low skill.

## 51.2 Separate strategic choice from execution

First assess recognition and execution of the level window's resource-conversion strategy, then its realization through distance, phase, movement and timing: understanding the matchup versus executing the choice.

\(K_h\) evaluates identifying favorable windows, using opposing resource commitments, preserving key abilities and converting trades into XP, gold or wave control. A single last-hit result is not its criterion.

\[
K_h=0.25K_{window}+0.20K_{resource\ plan}+0.15K_{cooldown\ discipline}
+0.15K_{cost\ conversion}+0.10K_{passive}+0.05K_{rune}
+0.10K_{adaptation}.
\]

\[
S_h^{two\text{-}layer}=\omega K_h+(1-\omega)O_h,
\qquad \omega\ge 0.60.
\]

\(O_h\) evaluates distance, phase, movement, attack cycles, resource costs and risk. Use small execution mistakes to distinguish players only after the strategy reaches a predefined threshold. Precise execution of a wrong strategy should not earn a high score.

## 51.3 Unified decision and scoring calculation

Passive and rune effects enter pre-action candidates through marginal value. \(U_{base}\) excludes effects already accounted for in gold, XP, health, mana and cooldown ledgers.

\[
Q_t^{+}(a)=E\left[
U_{base}(a)+\beta_P\Delta U^{passive}(a)
+\beta_R\Delta U^{rune}(a)+\beta_C\Delta U^{claim}(a)
\mid\Omega_t\right]
-\lambda\operatorname{CVaR}_{\alpha}(L(a))
+\nu\operatorname{VOI}_t(a)-\kappa C_{execute}(a).
\]

\(\Delta U^{passive}\) captures marginal stack, threshold, shield or buff effects. \(\Delta U^{rune}\) uses trigger success, frequency, availability and contribution. \(\Delta U^{claim}\) captures pressure during opposing resource commitments. Positive value requires a change to last hits, XP, position, state or the next event.

\[
E_{res,h}^{+}=\frac{\Delta G_h+\alpha\Delta X_h
+\beta_P\Delta R_h^{passive}+\beta_R\Delta R_h^{rune}}
{C_{HP,h}+C_{MP,h}+C_{CD,h}+\epsilon},
\]

\[
P_h^{+}=\frac{\Delta T_{resource,opponent}
+\rho_P\Delta T_{opponent}^{passive}
+\rho_R\Delta T_{opponent}^{rune}}
{C_{self}+\epsilon}.
\]

\[
O_h^{+}=0.35D_h+0.25E_{pos,h}+0.25E_{res,h}^{+}+0.15P_h^{+},
\]

\[
S_h^{final}=C_h\left[\omega K_h+(1-\omega)O_h^{+}\right],
\qquad \omega\ge0.60.
\]

Preserve the four scoring categories. Passive/rune effects enter resource efficiency through \(E_{res}^{+}\) and opposing pressure through \(P^{+}\), not as a second duplicate reward.

# 52. Output Format

At each decision node, output the task and shared endpoint; next target and collection method; commitment timing or trigger; cancellation conditions; pre-action candidate \(Q_t\) and margin over the next best; effect on the next last hit; insertable free time; both sides' gold/XP receipt efficiency and health/mana/cooldown spending; living unsettled resources; success probability or conditional range; and patch-dependent or uncertain inputs.

Use events as action-chain nodes, not only fixed timestamps. Update the next step when conditions change. Label every number as observed, parameter-derived or simulated.

# 53. Core Parameters

| Parameter | Meaning |
|---|---|
| \(t_0,T_{end},\mathcal M^{task}\) | Task boundaries and minion set |
| \(S,\Omega,G,\mathcal E\) | Local state, belief, attack graph and queue |
| \(K_i,\beta_c,\gamma_{minion\ attack}\) | Unit data, proportional minion damage and patch mechanics |
| \(\mathcal L,\mathcal C\) | Collectible impact and executable initiation sets |
| \(\mathcal F_A\) | Free time preserving last-hit constraints |
| \(P(b\mid\Omega,a),J\) | Opponent behavior and own short-term value |
| \(\ell_{behavior}\) | Difference in professional targets, order, timing and events |

### Parameter roles and scope

Not every variable must appear directly in the same objective. They enter policy evaluation through distinct paths:

| Role | Variables | Used in |
|---|---|---|
| State/transitions | \(S,\Omega,G,\mathcal E,K_i,\beta_c,\gamma_{minion\ attack}\) | State updates, damage, targeting, queue and candidate generation |
| Legality/deadlines | \(t_0,T_{end},\mathcal M^{task},\mathcal L,\mathcal C,\mathcal F_A\) | Windows, feasibility, joint scheduling, free time and termination |
| Value/search | \(W,P(b\mid\Omega,a),J,Q_t,V_t\) | Opponent branches, returns, risk adjustment, finite search and online selection |
| Search aids | \(\Phi_A,M_{h,i},\Delta M_i,VOI,I_{coupled}\) | Urgency, phase slack/changes, waiting and coupled-action admission |
| Post-action validation | \(\ell_{behavior}\) | Compare observed target, type, order, timing and waiting; excluded from pre-action \(Q_t\) |

Do not use \(\ell_{behavior}\) as an online reward: it requires future observed action labels. \(Q_t\) and \(V_t\) use only pre-action \(\Omega_t\), legal actions and probabilistic event branches.

# 54. Central Decision Test

> Under current observations and uncertainty, which legal action is most likely to make subsequent settlement of this minion group more favorable to our side?

Generate finite candidates from observable state, advance our actions, probabilistic opposing minion attacks and environmental events, and compare both sides' receipt efficiency, unsettled resources and execution costs. Select the current step before it happens and recompute after observations.

Professional recordings supply actual-action references. Deriving compatible targets, order and timing from pre-action state while explaining settlement supports local credibility.

The online choice is

\[
a_t^*\in\arg\max_{a\in\mathcal A_M(\Omega_t)}Q_t(a),
\]

not an explanation selected from outcomes after the action ends.

# 55. Final Definition

\[
\pi_A^*\in\arg\max_{\pi_A\in\Pi_M}
\left(E_{\Omega_{t_0},\pi_B,\mathcal E}[U_{t_0:T_{end}}]
-\lambda\operatorname{CVaR}_{\alpha}(L_{t_0:T_{end}})\right).
\]

\(\Pi_M\) is our legal policy set relevant to the present last-hit problem. Opposing policies and other-unit events define probabilistic transitions. Approximate evaluation with a finite event tree, execute one step and update from observations.

This definition applies to any local last-hit case with the required state and event rules. Special champions or modes are represented through data and transitions; the core algorithm, window tests and value principles remain consistent.
